Bill of materials

What to buy

Everything below is enough for one fish. Prices are approximate street prices in USD — check the Amazon links for current listings. Prefer an ESP32-S3 with PSRAM (8MB+ flash) for stable audio buffering.

Big Mouth Billy Bass (or clone)

The donor fish. Original Gemmy units and modern remakes both work — you need the mouth, head/body, and tail motors plus the speaker.

~$25–45 Amazon ↗

ESP32-S3 DevKit (N16R8 or similar)

ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 with 16MB flash + 8MB PSRAM recommended. Native USB (USB-OTG) simplifies flashing from Chrome.

~$10–18 Amazon ↗

INMP441 I²S MEMS Microphone

Digital mic module. Tie L/R to GND for left channel. Avoid analog electret mics — noise floor is worse near the motors.

~$5–10 Amazon ↗

MAX98357A I2S Amplifier (screw terminals preferred)

Class-D amp for the stock Billy speaker. Buy a breakout with screw terminals on the speaker side so you can clamp the snipped SPK wires without soldering.

~$5–10 Amazon ↗

L298N Dual H-Bridge Motor Driver

Drives two DC motors (mouth/head and tail). Use the 5 V logic supply from the onboard regulator when Vin is 7–12 V, or feed 5 V to the logic rail carefully.

~$4–8 Amazon ↗

5 V / 3 A USB-C power supply

Motors spike current when they stall. A phone charger often browns out — use a solid 5 V wall wart or a barrel jack into the L298N Vin with a shared ground.

~$10–15 Amazon ↗

Dupont jumper wires

Male-female jumpers for ESP32 header pins to mic, amp, and L298N logic inputs. No breadboard required once you know the pin map.

~$6–10 Amazon ↗

Lever nuts (Wago-style) or mini screw terminals

The no-solder joiners for Billy’s motor and speaker leads. 2-port and 3-port lever nuts let you splice stripped wires to pigtails that land on the L298N / amp.

~$8–15 Amazon ↗

USB-C data cable

Must support data (not charge-only) for Web Serial flashing. Many cheap cables fail here — keep a known-good one.

~$6–10 Amazon ↗

Optional: push button (wake / talk)

Momentary button to GPIO for push-to-talk if you prefer not to use the wake-word path on first boot.

~$5 Amazon ↗

Optional: 3D-printed backplate

Replaces the original plastic rear shell with mounts for ESP32 + L298N. Search MakerWorld for “Billy Bass backplate” (Thom Koopman / community designs).

~$0–15 MakerWorld ↗
Typical build total ≈ $75–120

Tools you’ll want

Phillips screwdriver, flush cutters, wire strippers, multimeter, zip ties or hot glue for strain relief. A soldering iron is optional on the bypass path — keep one handy only if a wire is too short or a module lacks terminals. Safety glasses when prying the shell.
